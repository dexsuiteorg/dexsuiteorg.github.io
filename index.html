Abstract

Human-level dexterity remains a central open problem in robotics, yet few simulation frameworks cover the full workflow of dexterous manipulation, from setting up a robot to collecting dexterous demonstrations and evaluating policies. We present DexSuite, an all-in-one simulation framework for dexterous manipulation built on the Genesis simulator. DexSuite provides 30 tasks that run with any of its 10 arms paired with any of its 12 end-effectors, from parallel-jaw grippers to dexterous hands, along with a standardized environment API, integrated teleoperation, and simulated tactile sensing. Moreover, we define a benchmark of 16 task-embodiment pairs spanning diverse contact complexities and horizons, and release a dataset of 4,700 successful episodes in simulation from human teleoperation, scripted experts, and learned policies. We release DexSuite as open source and hope others will build on it.

DexSuite Framework

DexSuite is a simulation framework built on top of Genesis. Genesis provides the underlying physics, rendering, asset loading, and parallel simulation, while DexSuite provides the manipulation layer needed to turn these primitives into reproducible robot learning experiments. This layer includes a library of tasks and robot embodiments, embodiment-independent task definitions, modular arm and end-effector composition, workspace-aware initialization, tactile sensing, teleoperation, and a unified Gymnasium API. DexSuite further provides a benchmark and curated dataset of dexterous manipulation demonstrations for training and evaluation.

Teleoperation

DexSuite supports teleoperation for both arm and finger control. Arm pose can be controlled with a SpaceMouse, HTC Vive, or Meta Quest controllers, while finger motion can be captured using MediaPipe, Leap Motion, Meta Quest hand tracking, or Manus MetaGlove Pro gloves.

Tactile sensing

DexSuite simulates the FlexiTac tactile sensor. A tactile pad is a grid of taxels on a flat elastomer, 12 by 32 at 2 mm spacing by default as on the real pad, and each taxel converts its penetration into nearby objects into a normal and a friction force. Pads can be declared on any end-effector link with a flat contact surface, with configurable grid size and spacing. We ship tactile pads on a UMI-style parallel-jaw gripper and on a tactile ALOHA manipulator, and the scripted gripper demonstrations record both pads at every step. The Sharpa demonstrations record per-finger contact forces instead.

Dataset

We release 4,700 successful episodes covering the 16 task-embodiment pairs of the benchmark, collected across three tiers: human teleoperation, scripted experts, and learned-policy rollouts. Together, these tiers support a range of data regimes within a single benchmark. The human tier comprises 700 demonstrations on the Robotiq, UMI, and Allegro embodiments. The scripted tier adds 1,200 demonstrations using the UMI gripper and Sharpa hand. The machine-generated tier contributes 2,800 successful policy rollouts.

All trajectories share a common observation and action format containing proprioception, object states, and front- and wrist-camera observations. Scripted tactile configurations additionally include fingertip tactile readings. The dataset is released in LeRobot format, as well as HDF5 format compatible with robomimic.

Benchmark

To address the three questions raised in the introduction, namely the embodiment gap, robustness to long horizons, and contact-rich capability, the benchmark contains 11 tasks organized into two evaluation tracks. Five tasks are simple enough to be attempted with either end-effector and are completed with both a parallel-jaw gripper and the Allegro hand. The other six are completed with a dexterous hand only, because their geometry precludes the use of a simple gripper. Four of them use the Allegro hand and the final two use the Sharpa hand due to their extreme geometric complexity. This gives 16 task-embodiment pairs.

Track 1. Cross-embodiment evaluation. The five shared tasks use identical task definitions and success criteria for both end-effectors. Track 2. Multi-fingered dexterous capability. The six hand-only tasks target stable grasping, constrained transport, tool use, multi-object interaction, and long-horizon manipulation.
