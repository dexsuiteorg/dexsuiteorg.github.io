DexSuite: An All-in-One Framework for Dexterous Manipulation Across Arms, Hands, and Tasks
Anas Houssaini, Hugo He, Junming Shi, Amin Abyaneh, Ricardo Chahine, Shuo Wen, Mohamad H. Danesh, Charles-Étienne Fréchette, Mariana Sosa Guzman, Mathias Desrochers, Rayan Houssaini, Adam Landa, Theophile Soulie, Charlotte Morissette, Annie Tims, Martino Russi, Jonathan Lussier, Gregory Dudek, Doina Precup, Julen Urain, Hsiu-Chin Lin, David Meger
arXiv:2610.37799

DexSuite brings the robot, the tasks, teleoperation, tactile sensing, the data and the evaluation into one stack. Any of its tasks runs with any arm and any hand.

Read the abstract

Human-level dexterity remains a central open problem in robotics, yet few simulation frameworks cover the full workflow of dexterous manipulation, from setting up a robot to collecting dexterous demonstrations and evaluating policies. We present DexSuite, an all-in-one simulation framework for dexterous manipulation built on the Genesis simulator. DexSuite provides 30 tasks that run with any of its 10 arms paired with any of its 12 end-effectors, from parallel-jaw grippers to dexterous hands, along with a standardized environment API, integrated teleoperation, and simulated tactile sensing. Moreover, we define a benchmark of 16 task-embodiment pairs spanning diverse contact complexities and horizons, and release a dataset of 4,700 successful episodes in simulation from human teleoperation, scripted experts, and learned policies. We release DexSuite as open source and hope others will build on it.
